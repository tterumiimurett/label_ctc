Instruction

Audio clips have been pre-labelled with a possible interruption where the first speaker may be stuck. Your task is to verify the candidate and annotate the interruption type.

Only evaluate the two highlighted utterances in each audio clip: the interrupted utterance and the interrupting utterance. The clip may contain other speech, but you do not need to label interruptions or stuck speech outside these highlighted turns.

Specifically, an interruption may happen when the original speaker gets stuck, and the other speaker helps by offering a word/phrase or asking a guiding question.

  1. First, decide whether this is a relevant interruption before the first speaker finishes. Choose Yes only if the second speaker comes in before the first speaker has finished what they were trying to say. Choose No for normal turn-taking, short expected replies, backchannels like "yeah" or "uh-huh", or unrelated interruptions/questions.
  2. Check whether the original speaker is stuck. Common signs include:
    • A long silent pause, e.g. >0.5s.
    • Repeated fillers such as "um", "uh", "well", or "you know".
    • Broken or restarted speech.
    • Repeated words.
    • Self-correction.
    • Stretched-out sounds while searching for a word.
  3. If the speaker is stuck, choose the interruption type. We are mainly interested in word/phrase completions and guiding questions; see the examples below.
  4. Drag the bold red line on the audio waveform to the end timestamp of the last stuck word before the other speaker steps in.
  5. If the speaker is stuck and interrupted, answer whether the word/phrase fits the speaker's intention and whether the interrupter becomes the main speaker.
  6. Correct the interrupted or interrupting transcript if they are wrong. For the interrupted transcript, remove words after the interruption.
