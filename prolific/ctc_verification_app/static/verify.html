Instruction

Audio clips have been pre-labelled with a possible interruption where the first speaker may be stuck. Your task is to verify the candidate and annotate the interruption type. Watch the task walkthrough.

Please annotate carefully: we manually review submissions and especially appreciate high-quality work, which may be rewarded. Submissions that appear careless, random, or inconsistent may be rejected and may lead to being blocked from future tasks.

Only evaluate the two highlighted utterances in each audio clip: the interrupted utterance and the interrupting utterance. The clip may contain other speech, but you do not need to label interruptions or stuck speech outside these highlighted turns.

Specifically, an interruption may happen when the original speaker gets stuck, and the other speaker helps by offering a word/phrase or asking a guiding question.

  1. Decide whether the second speaker completes the first speaker's unfinished sentence. Choose No for normal turn-taking, short expected replies, unrelated comments, or interruptions that do not complete the sentence. If the second speaker only gives a backchannel, such as "yeah" or "uh-huh", choose No. If No, you do not need to correct timestamps or transcripts.
  2. Decide whether the interrupted speaker is stuck before the other speaker steps in. Common signs:
    • A long silent pause, e.g. >0.5s.
    • Repeated fillers, for example:
      • Hesitation sounds: "um", "uh", "hmm", "erm", "er".
      • Planning words: "well", "so", "like", "I mean".
      • Interactive fillers: "you know", "you see", "right".
      • Thinking phrases: "let me see", "let me think", "how should I put it".
      • Uncertain phrases: "kind of", "sort of", "I guess", "maybe".
    • Broken or restarted speech.
    • Repeated words.
    • Self-correction.
    • Stretched-out sounds while searching for a word.
  3. Choose the interruption type. We are mainly interested in word/phrase completions and guiding questions. Choose Other only when the interruption is relevant but does not fit either type.
  4. Answer whether the interrupting utterance fits the speaker's intention. Skip this for guiding questions.
  5. Drag the bold red line to the end of the last real word before the other speaker steps in. Do not count fillers as the last word. The correct word end point may be after the blue range. If the red line is after the blue range, update the interrupted transcript so it ends at the marked word.
  6. Check the interrupted transcript in the left textbox. Remove words after the interruption and correct the text if needed. Then tick the checkbox.
  7. Drag the orange line to the true start of the interrupting utterance if needed. If there is a backchannel before the actual turn completion, do not include that backchannel in the start time. Place the orange line at the start of the word/phrase or guiding question that completes or helps the first speaker. Demo: backchannel before completion. The correct start may be before the orange range. If so, update the interrupting transcript so it starts at the marked word. Then tick the checkbox.
  8. Answer whether the interrupter becomes the main speaker after the interruption.
