Instruction

Audio clips have been pre-labelled with a possible interruption where the first speaker may be stuck. Your task is to verify the candidate and annotate the interruption type.

Please annotate carefully: we manually review submissions and especially appreciate high-quality work, which may be rewarded. Submissions that appear careless, random, or inconsistent may be rejected and may lead to being blocked from future tasks.

Only evaluate the two highlighted utterances in each audio clip: the interrupted utterance and the interrupting utterance. The clip may contain other speech, but you do not need to label interruptions or stuck speech outside these highlighted turns.

Specifically, an interruption may happen when the original speaker gets stuck, and the other speaker helps by offering a word/phrase or asking a guiding question.

  1. Decide whether the second speaker's utterance completes the first speaker's unfinished sentence. Choose Yes if the first speaker's incomplete phrase and the second speaker's response fit together into one complete, meaningful sentence or thought. Choose No for normal turn-taking, short expected replies, backchannels like "yeah" or "uh-huh", unrelated comments, or interruptions that do not complete the first speaker's sentence. If you choose No, you do not need to answer the remaining questions for this item.
  2. Decide whether the interrupted speaker is stuck before the other speaker steps in. Common signs include:
    • A long silent pause, e.g. >0.5s.
    • Repeated fillers such as "um", "uh", "well", or "you know".
    • Broken or restarted speech.
    • Repeated words.
    • Self-correction.
    • Stretched-out sounds while searching for a word.
  3. If the speaker is stuck, choose the interruption type. We are mainly interested in word/phrase completions and guiding questions; see the examples below.
  4. Answer whether the interrupting utterance correctly fits the speaker's intention. You can skip this for guiding questions.
  5. Drag the bold red line on the audio waveform to the end timestamp of the last word before the other speaker steps in. If the speaker is stuck, mark the last word after the stuck portion and before the interruption. If the speaker is not stuck, mark the last complete word before the interruption.
  6. Check the interrupted and interrupting transcripts. For the interrupted transcript, remove words after the interruption, then tick the transcript checkbox.
  7. Drag the orange line to the true start of the interrupting utterance if the pre-labelled start is wrong, then confirm that you checked it.
  8. Answer whether the interrupter becomes the main speaker after the interruption.
